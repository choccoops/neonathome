# Changelog

## 1.6.3

- **No treating yourself.** *Treat a critical injury* offered the character themselves as the patient (first, and by default). Cyberpunk RED (p.222): nobody can do a Treatment on themselves, though anyone can Quick Fix themselves. Now the patient list starts with the other characters, and the character only appears as "yourself: Quick Fix only" when they have an injury a Quick Fix cures for good (Torn Muscle, Foreign Object, Concussion), with only those injuries to pick.

## 1.6.2

- **Downtime actions always cost the character's downtime.** The GM could take a downtime action (Rest, the Hustle, Treatment, ...) for a character who wasn't taking part in Neon Hub's downtime, and it happened for free: a day of Rest healed without a day spent. Now every action needs the character in the downtime with the time it takes left, whoever clicks it, and it's logged in Neon Hub's downtime log; the buttons are off until then. Without Neon Hub, the GM's downtime switch has to be on. Maintenance and repairs work the same way when the GM keeps them to downtime.
- **Therapy takes the patient's week too**: they need to be in the downtime with a week left, and it goes in their log (through the GM when the Medtech's player doesn't own them).
- **Type the downtime to put into a project**: days, hours and minutes next to the slider, up to the downtime left (or what the project still needs). More than that shows in red and can't be confirmed, when starting a project or putting time in.
- **Rest and the Night Market say "Spend the time"**: there's nothing to roll.
- **Stripping a big batch for parts takes downtime.** A few items are still free; past the first 5 (a stack's units count one by one, materials being split or combined don't), each item takes 30 minutes, shown in the panel before you confirm.

## 1.6.1

- **The home stash is maintained too.** *Breaking Your Stuff* has everything a character owns maintained, so the Maintenance tab now lists the home stash's gear under *In the home stash*: covered by the character's Lifestyle and Role, due on their Rent Day (and counted on the Rent Day card), and maintained, inspected, repaired or marked by the GM like the rest. Working on it is done at home: the GM's downtime settings for the stash and for maintenance both apply. The Downtime tab's workshop lists it as well. Each item is still judged on its own price, never the total.
- **Any amount of downtime into a project.** Each project under way has a slider (in half hours, or type the hours) to put in exactly as much as the player wants, alongside the quick buttons; starting a Fabrication, Upgrade or Invention can put in an amount of the player's choosing too.

## 1.6.0

- **Maker work and repairs are projects.** Fabrication, Upgrade, Invention and repairs can take weeks or a month, so they're kept on the character with a meter, and carried over between downtimes. Downtime credits go into the meter; at the halfway mark the check comes up (the corebook: on a failure, "halfway through" the Tech realizes they have to start over, pp.140, 148-149). A failure empties the meter, the materials unharmed; a pass lets the rest of the time in; a full meter makes the item, applies the upgrade, writes up the invention, or gets the repaired item working.
- **The Projects panel** in the Downtime tab shows each project's meter with its halfway mark, where it stands (building to the check, check due, passed), restarts, and buttons to put in time (to the check, all of it, an hour, a day, a week), roll a due check, or abandon it.
- **Tech Materials.** Maker work is paid for in items, not eurobucks: *Cheap* through *Super Luxury Tech Materials*, in a new compendium. Fabrication takes materials a price category below the item, an Upgrade the item's own (p.148); they're used when the project starts, and the start card says where they came from. Each materials item's description has a *Salvaged from* line for the GM's books.
- **Strip for parts** (Techs): break down several items of any worth into one tier of materials (Damaged gear worth a category less, Destroyed two, from *Breaking Your Stuff*); stacks of materials break down or combine the same way. What's past the materials' worth is lost.
- **Salvage**, from R. Talsorian's *Salvaging Night City*: a week, a Basic Tech Check against the DLC's worth DVs, the category rolled or hunted, and a haul to pick out as Destroyed items of that category or as Tech Materials. A Critical Failure rolls the Perils of Salvaging, with the damage rolled, protective gear named, and a collapse drawing a Critical Injury to the Body. **2045** uses the DLC's places and tables; **2070** has the Night City of the *Edgerunner's Handbook*: one Combat Zone (Pacifica), no Hot Zone, and the Combat Zone, the Scrapyards, the Perimeter and the Badlands each with their own categories and perils. The era follows Neon Hub's lifestyle era, or a setting. All the tables are in a new *Salvage Tables* compendium, in Neon At Home's own words; an imported, edited copy is used in their place.
- **Repairs** from the Maintenance tab and the Downtime workshop start or carry on a repair project for whoever does the work, at the corebook's DV and time (p.140); a Tech adds Field Expertise.

## 1.5.0

- **The Biomonitor has a window of its own**, with its own button on the character sheet (owners and the GM) and in the Actors sidebar, out of the home's tabs.
- **Downtime.** A character is in downtime when Neon Hub has a downtime with them taking part (or, without Neon Hub, when the GM switches it on). The home's header shows it and the time left. Players can open their home any time; the GM chooses what waits for downtime: the home stash, the Weaponbench and armor upgrades, Vehicle Bay changes, and maintenance and repairs (all by default; the GM is never held back). When downtime starts, the players taking part get a card with a **Go home** button.
- **The Downtime tab** spends Neon Hub's downtime credits on the core rulebook's downtime actions, with Cyberpunk RED's own rolls and the character's eurobucks for materials: the **Hustle** (a week, 1d6 on the Role's table, paid by Role Ability Rank), **rest**, **treating critical injuries** (the injury's skill and DV; Cybertech on cyberlimbs), **maintenance and repairs**; for Techs, **Fabricate** (drop an item in), **Upgrade** and **Invent** with the Maker table and their Expertise; for Medtechs, **pharmaceuticals** (doses straight into their gear) and **therapy** for another character (Humanity restored, or an addiction); for Medias, **rumors** and **stories**; for Fixers, a monthly **Night Market**. Role actions appear for the character's Roles.
- **Maintenance and repairs in downtime** spend the time they take: the maintenance tier's time, and for repairs the corebook's DV and time by price (p.140; a destroyed vehicle, DV17 and a week), which the Repair dialog now starts from. A Tech adds Field Expertise to maintenance, inspection and repair checks.

## 1.4.2

- **Piles clear themselves.** A pile Neon At Home drops an item into goes away once the item is picked up out of it (Item Piles' own "delete when empty" can't tell on character-type piles, which carry Cyberpunk RED's skills and built-in cyberware). The pile token shows the dropped item.
- **A lost arm or hand limits what can be held.** With a Dismembered Arm or Hand (Cyberpunk RED's, or an offline cyberarm's), equipping a two-handed weapon, or a second one-handed weapon or shield when the one hand left is full, is stopped with a pop-up, from the character sheet and the Loadout alike. With both gone, nothing can be held. The GM can equip it anyway.
- **A lost limb takes its cyberware along.** A Dismembered Arm, Hand or Leg asks the GM which side, then drops what comes off with it where the character stands, in the same pile as what the hand held: a lost arm takes its cyberarm with every option in it (or the options in a meat arm), a lost hand its cyberhand, cyberfingers and other hand options (Rippers, Scratchers, Wolvers, Big Knucks, Subdermal Grip...), a lost leg its cyberleg and feet. A glove on that hand goes too. Torn off, the cyberware arrives Destroyed and has to be repaired with Cybertech before it's any use (Cyberpunk RED p.226); picked up, a cyberarm comes back with its options installed. On a cyberlimb, the injury gets a note that Cybertech can Quick Fix or Treat it in place of First Aid, Paramedic or Surgery (p.223). Without Item Piles, the cyberware just comes out of the body or the limb. A setting turns it off.
- **EMP lasts a minute**, as Cyberpunk RED's EMP ammunition and Microwaver have it (p.345): EMP'd gear comes back online once a minute of world time has passed (the GM can still bring it back sooner).
- **Gloves aren't limbs.** Cyberpunk RED installs the Battleglove and the smart gloves as foundational cyberarms; the Biomonitor no longer draws them as chrome arms, and an EMP'd or Destroyed glove isn't a lost arm.
- **Fixed:** the 1.4.1 fix for Cyberpunk RED's `Actor.create` now only ever touches Cyberpunk RED's own Actor classes.
- **Dropping into Item Piles checks Item Piles' setup first.** Item Piles makes piles as actors of the type in its System Specific settings ("Actor class type"). When that's blank (it can be, in worlds where Item Piles' Cyberpunk RED defaults were never applied) or isn't a Cyberpunk RED actor type, Item Piles fails with "Unsupported Entity type for create()" and "pileActor is undefined". The same goes for a blank "Item quantity attribute": Item Piles then counts everything in a pile as 0, so a dropped item couldn't be picked up again. In either case Neon At Home doesn't hand the item to Item Piles: the GM is told which setting to fix (reset Item Piles' System Specific settings to Cyberpunk RED's defaults), and the item is simply no longer carried.

## 1.4.1

- **Fixed: dropping into an Item Piles pile failed** ("pileActor is undefined") the first time Item Piles had to make its Default Item Pile actor. Cyberpunk RED's `Actor.create` doesn't hand back the actor it made for container actors (which Item Piles uses for piles), nor for mooks, demons or Black ICE; Neon At Home now makes it return the new actor, which also helps other modules that create those actors. Each failed attempt left an unconfigured "Default Item Pile" container actor in the Actors list: those can be deleted.
- **Fixed: no maintenance card on Rent Day.** The card relied on the GM's first check after loading the world to note where the calendar stood, and only counted characters with a player owner, so it could miss a Rent Day. Now every client notes each Neon Hub calendar's rent month as the world loads, and when a calendar passes Rent Day the GM posts one card for that calendar to the same players Neon Hub's own Rent Day card goes to (that calendar's players, or everyone), listing which player characters have gear to maintain, or that nothing is due. The GM's NPCs are never listed, and a calendar with no player characters on it posts nothing, so Rent Day doesn't flood the GM's chat.

## 1.4.0

- **Maintenance tab (Breaking Your Stuff).** A new tab built on R. Talsorian's *Breaking Your Stuff* DLC:
  - **Lifestyle maintenance.** Gear the character's Lifestyle covers (by its value, with drones and vehicles on their own scale) is maintained automatically. The Lifestyle comes from their active Neon Hub lifestyle package, in either era (2045 packages and their "+" versions, 2070 PrePaks), else the character sheet; the GM can set it by hand. Techs, Solos, Lawmen, Netrunners, Medias and Rockerboys count it a level higher for their trade's gear.
  - **Maintenance Checks** for everything else, once a month: the right Tech skill against DV13 to DV21, rolled with Cyberpunk RED's own skill roll, by the owner or anyone they pick. Success maintains it, clears Damage and ends a malfunction. Wear pips show months without maintenance.
  - **Maintenance months follow Neon Hub's Rent Day.** Each character's month is their Neon Hub calendar's rent month, like their Lifestyle bill: when the calendar passes Rent Day, their gear falls due and a card tells the players and GM who has what to maintain. The tab shows the rent month and days to Rent Day, and the GM can jump the calendar to it. Without calendars it follows Neon Hub's month counter; without Neon Hub (or by choice) the GM starts each month, or week.
  - **Damaged, Destroyed and Destroyed Beyond Repair** gear. Damaged gear works but can malfunction, and the GM can keep it quiet until a DV13 inspection finds it. Destroyed gear doesn't work: its effects switch off, its rolls are stopped, and Destroyed armor comes off and can't be worn until it's repaired. **Break gear** damages several pieces at once for one of the DLC's causes, picking the gear that cause affects. The bench shows what gear sells for in its condition.
  - **Malfunctions** from the DLC's eleven 1d6 tables (summarized in Neon At Home's own words, with the DLC's page): rolled by the GM, or automatically on a natural 1 with a Damaged item (an attack, a cyberdeck action). They last their time by world time, a Tech's Field Expertise can jury-rig them, and the card's button can drop the weapon, empty the magazine, mark it Destroyed and so on. A **Malfunction Tables** compendium has them all: import a table and edit it, and your copy is rolled.
- **Lost hands drop what they hold.** A Dismembered Arm or Hand (Cyberpunk RED's, or an offline cyberarm's) drops what that hand held: two-handed weapons always, everything if both arms are gone, and the GM picks for one-handed ones. With **Item Piles**, it lands in a pile beside the token with its attachments, which go back on when it's picked up.
- **Cyberware monitor in the Biomonitor.** A Vitals / Cyberware switch at the top of the tab. The cyberware view shows every piece installed in the body by type, in the character sheet's order (neuralware, cybereyes, cyberaudio, cyberarms, cyberlegs, internal, external, fashionware, borgware): each foundational piece or built-in container with its option slots as sockets (used and total, with slot upgrades), and what's installed in it, nested. A scan marks each piece on the body and draws cyberlimbs in chrome on their side (meat limbs stay flesh).
- **Cyberware conditions, as in Breaking Your Stuff.** The GM can mark any piece working, Damaged, Destroyed or Destroyed Beyond Repair, and knock it out with EMP (players see it, but can't change it). The scan and list show each in its colour, and the player and GM are told in chat. The Cyberware switch shows how many pieces are offline.
- **Offline cyberware doesn't work.** EMP'd or Destroyed cyberware, and everything installed in it, has its bonuses switched off and its weapons can't be rolled from the character sheet.
- **An offline cyberarm or cyberleg is a lost limb.** The character gets Cyberpunk RED's Dismembered Arm or Dismembered Leg critical injury, with its effects (e.g. -6 MOVE), named for the cause ("Dismembered Leg (Destroyed Cyberleg)") and shown on that side of the trauma map. It's removed by itself when the limb works again or is uninstalled, and doesn't raise the Death Save penalty. Meat limbs aren't affected.
- **Fixed: saving an item's Neon At Home settings could clear settings the form doesn't show**, such as a vehicle's cargo capacity.

## 1.3.1

- **Fixed: the character sheet wouldn't open** ("can't access property \"changes\"") after Encumbered or Overloaded was applied through Condition Lab & Triggler, or placed from the token HUD. Cyberpunk RED's sheet needs its own bookkeeping on every effect change, and the MOVE penalty added to those effects didn't have it. Every effect carrying the penalty now does, however it's made, and effects already broken in your world are repaired when the GM loads it.

## 1.3.0

- **Subdermal Pockets give carry slots.** Each Subdermal Pocket installed as cyberware adds 2 bonus slots that take gear/utility or a one-handed weapon, shown in the Loadout like a Gear bag (two pockets: two bars, numbered). It's found by name, so the core item works as is; an uninstalled pocket gives nothing.
- Only the Subdermal Pocket does this; no other cyberware gives carry slots. The GM can change a pocket's slots in its item settings.
- **The "No room to carry it" pop-up explains bonus slots that didn't help.** When a bonus pool still has room but only takes other things (a Gear bag offered a pistol, a holster offered a rifle), the pop-up now says so, e.g. "Gear bag has 2 free, but only for Gear / utility."

## 1.2.2

- **Fixed: cyberware installed in the body took carry slots and weight.** Cyberpunk RED lists cyberware installed in the body on the character itself rather than inside another item, and the Loadout didn't look there, so each piece counted as a hidden 1-slot, 0.5-unit item. It also filled gear-only bonus slots like the Gear bag. Installed cyberware is now free, and any weight-limit bonus it gives applies.
- The carry summary names the bonus slots that only take certain things, e.g. "+2 Gear bag (Gear / utility only)".

## 1.2.1

- **Carry bar colours always show.** Used slots are filled in their kind's colour (set on the bar itself, so no other stylesheet can grey them out), brighter, against a darker empty track.
- **Encumbered and Overloaded icons are full size on tokens.** They're now 512×512 like Cyberpunk RED's own status icons, filling the frame; before, without a set size, they drew small. If you made Condition Lab conditions with these icons, they pick up the new ones automatically.

## 1.2.0

- **Carry slots are a hard limit.** Carrying or equipping something that would go past a character's carry slots is stopped with a pop-up saying what doesn't fit and why, in the Loadout and with the Cyberpunk RED sheet's equip toggle. The GM's pop-up can carry it anyway. Things arriving on a character (bought, looted, dropped on the sheet) aren't refused, so nothing paid for is lost; the pop-up says they're over. Fitting an upgrade or attachment from the stash isn't stopped by full slots, since it costs nothing once installed. The setting still offers warn or off.
- **Encumbrance.** Over the weight limit MOVE is halved (rounded down, at least 1); over double the limit MOVE is 1 and the GM may call for checks. It's applied after other effects, the player and GM are told in chat when it changes, the Loadout shows it under the weight bar, and the GM can turn it off in the Weight rules.
- **Encumbered and Overloaded status effects**, with their own icons, shown on the token and in the token HUD (placing one by hand applies the MOVE penalty too, and the automatic check leaves it alone).
- **Condition Lab & Triggler.** If Condition Lab has conditions named Encumbered and Overloaded (names set in the module settings), Neon At Home applies and removes them through CLT, so their macros, chat and Triggler triggers run, and adds the MOVE penalty to them.
- **Lifestyle items are left out of the Loadout.** Neon Hub's lifestyle items (Kibble, apartments, memberships, transit...) are monthly expenses, not things you carry: they no longer take carry slots, weigh nothing, and don't appear on you, on the shelf or in storage, even when equipped.
- **Clearer carry bar.** One bar with a tick per slot, filled in colour by kind (weapons, ammo, gear...), with a key of how many slots each kind uses, how many are free, and how many over (with a mark where the slots end). Bonus slots (holster, ammo bag) get the same smaller bar.

## 1.1.0

- **Trauma map on the Biomonitor.** Each critical injury is marked where it is on the body: eyes, ears, jaw, throat, skull and brain, lungs, ribs, spine, arms, hands and legs. Homebrew injuries with similar names land in the right place; anything else goes to the head or torso. Markers pulse, and grow and redden as injuries stack at one spot (with a ×2, ×3 count). Hover one for the injuries there and their quick fix; click it to open the injury.
- **Lost limbs show.** A dismembered arm, hand or leg is blacked out and cut on the figure; a lost eye or ear is marked with an ✕.
- **Left and right.** Injuries to paired parts spread across both sides; the arrows button in the injury list moves one to the other side.
- **Trauma level** (Stable, Injured, Critical, Failing) from how many injuries there are and how many raise the Death Save. Critical and Failing redden the scan and make the figure flicker. Head and body light up more strongly as their injuries stack.
- **Opening the window:** a second click while the window is still opening no longer starts it again, and if opening fails it says so (with the details in the console).

## 1.0.0

First release: a home suite for each Cyberpunk RED character, opened from the character sheet.

- **Loadout.** Carry slots by worn armor class from the Armor & Carrying 2.0 homebrew (10 to 42), slot costs by kind (heavy weapons 4, two-handed 3, one-handed 2, ammo and gear 1), bonus slots from carry gear and armor upgrades, and a weight limit modelled on MMuton's Weight System. Equip, carry or store items; a home stash (a Cyberpunk RED container actor) and vehicle cargo as storage, with drag and drop. The GM chooses to show, warn or block going over.
- **Armor upgrades.** The spreadsheet's upgrades in a compendium, limited by armor class and location, installed into the upgrade slots each class gives. Worn upgrades keep an *armor fit* effect on the character (penalty relief, Plate Padding, War Paint, AI Armor Interface).
- **Carry gear.** Shoulder Holster, Weapon Sling, Carryall Bag, Trench Coat and Armored Trench Coat.
- **Vehicle Bay.** Bays from the parking of the character's Neon Hub home or household (or set by the GM); vehicle actors and vehicle items parked in them or on the street; the ride in use; HP, SP, speed, crew seats, mounted weapons, upgrades and cargo from MMuton's Vehicle Actor Sheet.
- **Biomonitor.** HP and wounds, armor, critical injuries, Humanity and EMP, and the Syndromes & Inspiration homebrew: Empathy tiers, the thirteen Syndromes and their Stressors (plus the GM's own and custom ones), dormant Syndromes and scars, Catharsis (gained on falling a tier, taking Humanity Loss first), Antipathy, In The System, a complete break, Inspiration and Intensive Therapy.
- **Weaponbench.** A weapon's attachment slots, its numbers with and without attachments, what each attachment does, attaching from the character or the stash, detaching (except fixed ones), and custom attachments.
- **Settings.** Carry & Weight Rules (every number from the spreadsheet, editable, with a reset), item settings for the GM on every item sheet, and an API for macros and new sub-features.
